                                                   4. Harman’s sieve
In this section we apply Harman’s sieve, constructing a function ρ(n; x) which is a minorant of the prime
indicator function 1P (n) for each x, and which has exponent of distribution θ = 0.5253 = 1/2 + 2 ·0.01265.

4.1. Motivation. We first explain our choice of θ. In the arguments of Maynard [8] and Polymath [14],
bounds on Hm were derived using the exponent of distribution of Λ(n). This exponent of distribution
was determined by decomposing Λ(n) via the Heath-Brown identity and treating various convolutions
of coefficient sequences separately. Certain bad coefficient sequences, corresponding to convolutions of 5
sequences of length about x0.2 , formed a barrier to improving the exponent of distribution of Λ(n) further.
The idea behind a use of Harman’s sieve is to discard these bad convolutions: Baker and Irving [2] noticed
that if we replace Λ(n) by log(x)ρω (n; x), where ρω (n; x) is a minorant for 1P (n) which satisfies

4.1.1. Limitations. Somewhat unusually, this particular application of Harman’s sieve has a discontinuity,
present in the same form both in Baker and Irving’s and in our work: The minorant ρω (n; x) is constructed
using the Buchstab identity, and in the process one must consider whether or not α1 ⋆ α2 ⋆ α3 with
αi (n) = 1P (n) on [Ni , 2Ni ] and N1 N2 N3 ≍ x has exponent of distribution 1/2 + 2ω. To do so, each αi is
first decomposed further via the Heath-Brown identity and we consider αi = ψi ⋆ βi , where ψi and βi have
support [Ai , 2Ai ] and [Bi , 2Bi ], Ai Bi ≈ Ni , Bi ≤ x0.1 and ψi (n) = 1 on [Ai , 2Ai ]. Good equidistribution
estimates are available if A1 A2 A3 is large or if the product of some subset of {A1 , A2 , A3 , B1 , B2 , B3 } is
close to x0.5 . In particular, if ω = 1/79 then we either need that A1 A2 A3 ≥ x0.8925 or that a product
of A1 , A2 , A3 , B1 , B2 , B3 is contained in [x0.405 , x0.595 ], and one of these two cases is always satisfied by
ψ1 ⋆ ψ2 ⋆ ψ3 ⋆ β1 ⋆ β2 ⋆ β3 .

However, as soon as ω is increased slightly beyond 1/79, our equidistribution estimates do not cover
the case where A1 A2 A3 is very close to x0.8925 – the three smooth factors are too short for Polymaths’s
Type III estimate, but too long for our Type I/II estimate. When constructing ρω (n; x), this forces us
to discard many products of three primes in Buchstab’s identity, leading to a sudden jump where c1 (ω)
becomes much larger as ω increases beyond 1/79.

This discontinuity determines the choice ω = 0.01265 < 1/79. Here we still have c1 (ω) < 2 · 10−5 and
thus obtain the proposed bound Hm = O(exp(3.8075m)).
